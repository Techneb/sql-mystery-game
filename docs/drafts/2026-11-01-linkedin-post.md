I teach SQL in Paris. My students learn the syntax fast. Reading the rows a query returns, and asking whether they are the right ones, takes them much longer.

So I made The Ritz Affair, a free detective game you play in SQL. Paris, May 1912: someone at the Ritz is missing a great blue stone, and all you have is the police archive. Each of the twelve chapters needs one construct, from a first WHERE to a recursive CTE. It runs in the browser, and when a query goes wrong it tells you why in one plain sentence.

Beside it sits The Clerk's Handbook: fourteen short lessons on a small bakery database and twenty graded exercises you can try in a terminal on the same page.

About 4,400 people opened the game in October, most of them after Software with Nick showed it on YouTube. Thank you, Nick. Many never ran a single query, so chapter I now opens with a short guided warm-up.

The game: https://alephb.uk/ritz-affair/
The handbook: https://alephb.uk/learn/

Teachers and learners, I would love to hear how it goes for you.

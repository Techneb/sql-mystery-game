Six ways to paint Lord Ashcombe

Follow-up to illustrations.html section 7. The silhouette portraits are out; the suspects get painted portraits with a high level of detail. This board renders one sitter, drawn once, through six treatments, each in the day edition (cream paper) and the night edition (the same file under one CSS filter, as the site would do it). What differs between the six is exactly what a style choice fixes: palette, light, finish and surface.

These are procedural stand-ins, not paintings: a shaded vector bust pushed through SVG filters for canvas weave, brush displacement, a single gaslight, autochrome grain and pastel bloom. They show the register of each style faithfully; the real assets, commissioned or generated from the prompt under each one, would carry the fine detail (skin, whiskers, cloth) that no filter adds. Choose the register here, judge the detail on the first real portrait.

The six treatments

same sitter, same pose, same key light, in every plate
A · GRISAILLE OIL · DAY
A · GRISAILLE OIL · NIGHT

A. Grisaille oil

Monochrome oil in raw umber or Payne's grey; look at Ingres's underpaintings, Van Dyck's grisaille sketches, and the grey-brown reproductions in L'Illustration.

Full old-master modelling in a single hue. Because the source carries no colour of its own, the night edition is one filter with nothing to fight: this is the only style where day and night come out as one coherent picture rather than a tinted photograph. Sits on cream paper and prints on the certificate without a second version.

Bust portrait of an English peer, about 55, 1912, three-quarter view, receding grey-brown hair, full moustache, wing collar, dark frock coat. Grisaille oil painting in raw umber monochrome, smooth glazes, fine canvas weave visible, single soft key light from upper left, neutral expression, dark plain ground. Museum reproduction, no frame, no text.
Recommended. Cheapest to maintain (one file per sitter, one filter), most at home with the ink drawings around it.
B · ACADEMIC · DAY
B · ACADEMIC · NIGHT

B. Belle Epoque academic portrait

Leon Bonnat, Carolus-Duran, Jean-Jacques Henner, Jules Lefebvre: the Salon portraitists of the Third Republic.

Deep ground, chiaroscuro, tight rendering of skin, whiskers and starched linen, formal dress and a formal face. Reads as the official portrait a prefecture would keep in a file, which is exactly what the case board pretends to be. Full colour, so the night filter turns it cold and photographic; acceptable, less elegant than A.

Formal oil portrait in the manner of Leon Bonnat, 1912. Bust of an English peer, about 55, three-quarter view, receding hair, full moustache, wing collar, dark frock coat, small red enamel pin. Dark brown ground, strong chiaroscuro from upper left, highly finished skin and linen, restrained warm palette, neutral expression. Museum reproduction, no frame, no text.
C · SOCIETY BRAVURA · DAY
C · SOCIETY BRAVURA · NIGHT

C. Society bravura

John Singer Sargent, Giovanni Boldini, Antonio de La Gandara, Philip de Laszlo: the painters the Ritz's guests actually sat for.

The face and hands finished tight, everything else laid in with fast, visible, directional strokes. Glamour and speed: this is Lupin's world, and it is the one style that gives each suspect a temperament through brushwork alone. Most demanding of the artist or the model; the loose passages must still read at 60 px on the case board.

Society portrait in the manner of John Singer Sargent and Boldini, 1912. Bust of an English peer, about 55, three-quarter view, receding hair, full moustache, wing collar, dark frock coat. Face finely finished; coat, collar and background in loose, confident, visible brushstrokes; dark warm ground with a single soft key light from upper left; neutral expression. Museum reproduction, no frame, no text.
D · TENEBRIST · DAY
D · TENEBRIST · NIGHT

D. Tenebrist

Rembrandt, Georges de La Tour, Ribera; in 1912 terms, the "Rembrandt lighting" of the Nadar and Reutlinger photographic studios.

One warm source, upper left, and the face emerging from near-black. It is the desk lamp and the gaslight already in the spec's palette, so it ties the portraits to the room the student is sitting in, and the night edition barely has to change. The cost: on cream paper by day a very dark rectangle is heavy, and six of them on a board is a wall.

Tenebrist oil portrait in the manner of Rembrandt, 1912 sitter. Bust of an English peer, about 55, three-quarter view, receding hair, full moustache, wing collar, dark frock coat. Single warm candle-like light from upper left, face and collar lit, everything else falling into deep brown-black shadow, fine grain, neutral expression. Museum reproduction, no frame, no text.
E · AUTOCHROME · DAY
E · AUTOCHROME · NIGHT

E. Autochrome tint

The Lumiere brothers' autochrome plates (1907 onward); Albert Kahn's Archives de la Planete; the Gervais-Courtellemont Paris portraits.

Not a painting: a colour photograph the way 1912 could make one, with the dyed starch grain that gives autochromes their pointillist shimmer and muted, slightly wrong colour. The most period-exact detailed likeness a newspaper could have run, and the only style that says "evidence" rather than "portrait". Day and night both work; the grain hides generation artefacts well.

Autochrome Lumiere colour photograph, 1912, studio portrait. Bust of an English peer, about 55, three-quarter view, receding hair, full moustache, wing collar, dark frock coat. Soft daylight from upper left, muted desaturated colour, visible fine coloured starch grain, slight softness, plain dark backdrop, neutral expression. Glass plate scan, no frame, no text.
F · PASTEL · DAY
F · PASTEL · NIGHT

F. Pastel

Maurice Quentin de La Tour, Rosalba Carriera; nearer 1912, Degas's and Helleu's pastel heads.

Powdery, luminous, highly worked faces with the paper's tooth showing through. The softest and lightest of the six; it flatters the Comtesse and sits beautifully on cream. It is also the least like a police file, and the night filter dulls the chalk to plaster. A candidate for the victim's portrait alone, if styles were mixed.

Pastel portrait on toned paper in the manner of Quentin de La Tour, 1912 sitter. Bust of an English peer, about 55, three-quarter view, receding hair, full moustache, wing collar, dark frock coat. Soft powdery modelling, visible paper tooth, luminous highlights, light warm palette, neutral expression, plain ground. Museum reproduction, no frame, no text.

How the chosen style ships

Assets. One WebP per sitter (six suspects, Ganimard, the Comtesse; Lupin himself never), about 600 x 800 px and 40 to 80 KB each. Commissioned from one brief, or generated from the prompt above with only the sitter's line changed, so pose, light and finish stay constant across the cast and no face reads guiltier than another. Night. The same file under the CSS filter these plates use (brightness(.72) sepia(.45) hue-rotate(188deg) saturate(1.15)); no second set. On the paper. An oval or rounded-corner vignette, a halftone overlay in mix-blend-mode: multiply, and a hairline rule, so the portrait reads as a reproduction in the newspaper rather than a photo pasted on it. On the case board. The same file at about 60 px with the halftone stronger; styles C and F should be checked at that size before the whole cast is produced.

3

Decisions

  1. Style. A to F. Two can be combined (one for the file portraits, another for the Comtesse alone), at the cost of a second brief.
  2. Source. Commissioned or generated. Either way the first delivery should be Ashcombe alone, judged at chapter-header size and at case-board size, before the rest of the cast.
  3. Blakeney. A face from chapter I like everyone else, or only after chapter VIII names him.
  4. Where. Chapter header only, case board only, or both.

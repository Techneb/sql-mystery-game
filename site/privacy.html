What the paper knows about you

Privacy notice — last updated 9 October 2026

The Ritz Affair is a browser game for learning SQL. It has no accounts. The whole game, database included, runs in your browser. Advertising may appear on the page's edges, outside the game, once Google's review of the site is complete, and only with your consent (below). The game sets no cookies and loads nothing from Google unless you allow usage statistics or advertising.

Investigate (learning mode)

Your progress, notes, queries, badges and day/night choice are saved in your browser's local storage (ritz.learn, ritz.compete, ritz.theme) so you can pick up where you left off. Only you can see them; clearing this site's data in your browser erases them. None of it is sent anywhere.

Usage statistics (only if you say Yes)

The game page asks once: Help improve the course: allow anonymous usage statistics? Your answer is kept in local storage (ritz.consent). Until you answer Yes, and whenever you answer No, nothing is loaded from Google and no analytics cookie is set. You can change your mind at any time from the Statistics link at the bottom of the game page; choosing No deletes the _ga cookies.

If you say Yes, the page uses Google Analytics 4:

  • It sets the first-party cookies _ga and _ga_<id>, which Google keeps for up to 13 months by default. The collected data itself is kept for 14 months, then deleted.
  • Google receives page views: the page address and referrer, and your browser, device model and approximate location (country, region, city) as Google Analytics works them out from the connection. Google Analytics 4 does not log or store IP addresses.
  • And these game events, with only these fields:
    • chapter_open: chapter number, mode (learn or compete);
    • chapter_solve: chapter number, mode, seconds taken, number of queries, number of wrong answers;
    • game_finish: part, mode;
    • badge: badge name.

Never sent to Google: your team name or pseudo, your answers, your SQL queries, your notes.

Why: to improve the course — which chapters students drop out of, how long each takes, and to tune the difficulty and the ranks. Google signals and ad personalisation are off, and the data is not used for advertising. Google processes it on the course owner's behalf, and may do so in the United States under the EU-US Data Privacy Framework.

Advertising

At the time of writing, no ad script is loaded: the site is waiting for Google AdSense to review it. Once ads are switched on, they appear in a box beside the game, never inside a chapter, and this is what they involve:

  • Third-party vendors, including Google, use cookies to serve ads based on your prior visits to this site or to other sites.
  • Google's use of advertising cookies enables it and its partners to serve ads to you based on your visits to this site and/or other sites on the Internet.
  • You may opt out of personalised advertising in Google's Ads Settings, or opt out of other vendors' use of cookies for personalised advertising at www.aboutads.info.

Advertising is asked for separately from usage statistics, in its own consent message. If you refuse it, you see ads that are not personalised from your browsing, or none. The Statistics link at the bottom of the game page only reopens the statistics question; to change your advertising choice, clear this site's data in your browser and the consent message appears again on your next visit.

Compete

When your teacher gives you a season link and you press Start the clock, the game sends the following to the class leaderboard, at the start, after each chapter and at the finish:

  • the pseudo you type,
  • the season number and the chapter you reached,
  • how many queries you ran and wrong answers you gave,
  • your browser's clock reading at each message and, at the finish, the time you took by that clock.

Each answer you submit in compete mode is also sent, in its simplified form, so the leaderboard can check it against the season's answers; it is checked and not stored. The leaderboard adds the time it received each message and gives your browser a code tied to your pseudo, so that nobody else can post under it afterwards. It never receives your queries or your notes. It is a Google Sheet belonging to the course teacher, reached through Google Apps Script, so Google processes it under its own terms. Anyone with a leaderboard link sees the pseudos and times of the season that link names; the teacher sees every season. The rows stay until the teacher deletes them. This is separate from usage statistics and does not depend on your answer to the banner.

Choose a pseudo that is not your full name. A first name or a nom de guerre is enough.

What the page loads from elsewhere

  • The site is hosted on GitHub Pages, which, like any web host, may log your IP address.
  • Fonts come from Google Fonts, and the SQL engine (sql.js) from cdnjs; both see your IP address when your browser fetches them.
  • Only if you allow usage statistics: the Google Analytics script from googletagmanager.com, and its measurements sent to google-analytics.com.
  • Once advertising is switched on (not yet): Google's ad and consent scripts and the ads themselves, from Google's ad servers.

What the paper knows about you

Privacy notice — last updated 14 November 2026

The Ritz Affair is a browser game for learning SQL. You need no account to play it; signing in with Google, only to carry your progress to another device, is optional (see Saved progress). The whole game, database included, runs in your browser. It is served from alephb.uk/ritz-affair/ and its handbook, The Clerk's Handbook, from alephb.uk/learn/; this notice covers both. The page shows advertising from Google AdSense in one box under the schema beside the game, never inside a chapter, and in the same box under the schema of the handbook's exercises page. In the European Economic Area, the United Kingdom and Switzerland, Google's consent message asks on your first visit whether Google and its partners may use cookies and your data for advertising and for usage statistics, once Google has made it active for this site; until then, and whenever it cannot load, the game asks its own question about usage statistics. Elsewhere both are on by default and you can opt out (below). The game itself sets no cookies.

Investigate (learning mode)

Your progress, notes, queries, badges and day/night choice are saved in your browser's local storage (ritz.learn, ritz.compete, ritz.theme) so you can pick up where you left off; the handbook, the Clerk's Desk and the leaderboard read and write the same day/night choice (ritz.theme). Only you can see them; clearing this site's data in your browser erases them. None of it is sent anywhere, unless you sign in (see Saved progress).

Until 31 October 2026 the game lived at mystery.alephb.uk. That address now only forwards you here. On the way, its page reads what your browser kept for it (the keys starting with ritz. listed in this notice) and hands them to the game in the part of the address after #, which your browser never sends to any server. The game then copies each one into this site's storage, unless this site already holds that key (a saved game is replaced only by one further along), and removes it from the address.

The handbook's exercises page works the same way: your queries run in your browser, and the list of questions you have solved and its own query history are kept in its local storage (ritz.exercises, ritz.exercises.history, separate from the game's), never sent unless you sign in (see Saved progress). It carries the same advertising box as the game (see Advertising). Every handbook page asks the game's question about usage statistics and loads them under the same rules (see Usage statistics). One answer, kept in ritz.consent, covers the game and the handbook.

Your consent

The page tells Google's tags a default for each purpose (Google Consent Mode: ad storage, ad user data, ad personalisation and analytics storage), and Google applies the one that fits your country:

  • In the European Economic Area, the United Kingdom and Switzerland, every purpose starts as denied. On your first visit a message from Google (Google's certified consent management platform, served by the AdSense script) asks for your consent to advertising and usage statistics, with buttons to accept or refuse and a way to manage each purpose. Google keeps your choice in its own cookies on this site. Until you choose, and whenever you refuse, every purpose stays denied.
  • Everywhere else, no message from Google is shown and every purpose starts as granted: usage statistics are counted and the ads may be personalised, until you opt out.

Google shows its message only once it has made it active for this site, and the AdSense script may be blocked. So the page waits up to 3 seconds after that script loads (not at all if it fails). If Google's message is not active by then, a short question from the game itself, at the bottom of the page, asks every visitor who has not yet answered it whether to allow anonymous usage statistics (Yes or No). Your answer is kept in your browser's local storage (ritz.consent). It covers statistics only: in Europe the advertising purposes then stay denied. If Google's message appears later, it replaces the game's question and decides from then on; the two are never shown together.

From a browser set to a European time zone (the page cannot see your country, so the time zone stands in for it), nothing is sent to Google Analytics before a Yes, in Google's message or in the game's question: its script is not even loaded.

You can change your mind at any time: the Privacy and cookie settings link at the bottom of the game's front page (Statistics at the bottom of the handbook's pages) reopens Google's message where it asks about statistics (Europe), and the game's question everywhere else. If you withdraw consent to statistics, the page stops sending them and deletes the _ga cookies. Outside Europe, opt out of personalised ads in Google's Ads Settings (more below), and of statistics with that link, Google's Google Analytics opt-out add-on or your browser's tracking protection.

Usage statistics

The game and the handbook load Google Analytics 4 at once outside Europe's time zones. In a European time zone it is loaded only after your Yes (above), so until then nothing at all reaches Google Analytics. If your time zone is outside Europe but Google places you in Europe, the analytics default there is denied: until you consent it sets no cookie and sends only cookieless signals (the page viewed and the game events below, with no identifier tying them to you), which Google uses to estimate overall numbers. With statistics on (your Yes in Europe, the default elsewhere):

  • It sets the first-party cookies _ga and _ga_<id>, which Google keeps for up to 13 months by default. The collected data itself is kept for 14 months, then deleted.
  • Google receives page views: the page address and referrer, and your browser, device model and approximate location (country, region, city) as Google Analytics works them out from the connection. Google Analytics 4 does not log or store IP addresses.
  • And these game events, with only these fields:
    • chapter_open: chapter number, mode (learn or compete);
    • chapter_solve: chapter number, mode, seconds taken, number of queries, number of wrong answers, and how you reached the answer as a fixed category: same_result (one of your queries in that chapter returned it in a single row), found (one showed it among other rows) or unseen (none of them showed it). Your queries and the answer are never sent;
    • chapter_queries: chapter number, mode, and a count (1, 5 or 20), sent once when you reach that many queries in a chapter you have not solved yet. The queries themselves are never sent;
    • game_finish: part, mode;
    • badge: badge name;
    • chapter_leave: chapter number, mode, seconds since the chapter opened and number of queries, sent once when you hide or close the page before solving it;
    • query_fail: chapter number, mode, whether the query failed or was refused, the name of the rule it broke (such as group-by) and the kind of error (such as syntax or no_table, a fixed category, never the query or a name you typed), for the first three failed queries of a chapter;
    • answer_wrong: chapter number, mode, and 1 or 3, sent on your first and third wrong answer in a chapter. The answer itself is never sent;
    • step_solve: chapter number, mode, step, seconds taken, number of queries and number of wrong answers, sent when you find chapter I's date. The date you typed is never sent;
    • hint_open: chapter number, mode, sent the first time you open a chapter's Hint;
    • hint_where: chapter number, mode (and, in chapter I, the step), sent the first time you open a Hint's Where to look;
    • landing_begin: mode, when you press Investigate or Join on the front page;
    • feature_use: the name of a feature you used and the chapter number (and the part, for a film), once per visit: enlarging the schema, opening the suspects, reusing a query from History, following a refusal's Grammar link, playing or finishing a film, opening the front page's About, switching day and night, finishing or skipping the clerk's first morning (the practice page before chapter I), finishing or skipping chapter I's short tour, clicking the house advertisement for the handbook or the front page's warm-up link, reopening a solved chapter from the case board, sending a message with the Feedback form, and pressing a share button at the end of the game (with which one: X, LinkedIn, Copy link or your device's Share).
  • And these handbook events, with only these fields:
    • exercise_run: the exercise's name, its level, and whether the run worked, failed or was refused, for the first run of each exercise in a visit;
    • exercise_solve: the exercise's name, its level and how many times you ran a query on it in this visit;
    • exercise_level: the level whose button you pressed;
    • query_fail: the exercise's name, whether the query failed or was refused, the rule's name and the kind of error (a fixed category, as above), for the first three failed queries of an exercise in a visit;
    • feature_use: the name of a feature you used, once per visit: enlarging the schema, reusing a query from History, following a Grammar link, opening How to use this page or a solution, following a Try it link (with the exercise's name), pressing Try it here on a lesson page, switching day and night, finishing or skipping the exercises page's short tour, clicking the handbook's own advertisement, sending a message with the Feedback form.

The share buttons at the end of the game open X or LinkedIn in a new tab with the share text, and nothing is sent to them until you press one. Copy link only puts the address on your clipboard.

Never sent to Google: your team name or pseudo, your answers, your SQL queries, your notes, anything you type. The Feedback form below is the one thing you type that leaves the page, and only to the course owner.

Why: to improve the course. The figures show which chapters students drop out of and how long each takes, and they tune the difficulty and the ranks. Google signals and ad personalisation are off, and the data is not used for advertising. Google processes it on the course owner's behalf, and may do so in the United States under the EU-US Data Privacy Framework.

To withdraw or change your advertising consent in Europe, use the Privacy and cookie settings link at the bottom of the game's front page, which reopens Google's message; in US states with their own privacy laws (California and others), Google shows a Do Not Sell or Share My Personal Information link on the page; elsewhere, use Google's Ads Settings or aboutads.info above. Compete

When your teacher gives you a season link, the page asks the class leaderboard for that season's name and state, and asks again every 5 seconds while you wait for the teacher to open the session. When you press Join, it sends your pseudo, the season's identifier and your browser's clock reading. After each chapter and at the finish it sends:

  • your pseudo and the season's identifier,
  • the chapter you solved,
  • how many queries you ran and wrong answers you gave,
  • your browser's clock reading and the time since the session opened by that clock.

Your answers are checked in your browser and never sent. The leaderboard adds the time it received each message and gives your browser a code tied to your pseudo, so that nobody else can post under it afterwards. It never receives your queries or your notes. It is a Google Sheet belonging to the course teacher, reached through Google Apps Script, so Google processes it under its own terms. Anyone holding a season or leaderboard link can read the pseudos, chapters and times of that season; the other seasons are readable only by the teacher. The rows stay until the teacher deletes them (deleting a season erases all its rows). This is separate from usage statistics and does not depend on your answer to the consent message.

Choose a pseudo that is not your full name. A first name or a nom de guerre is enough.

Feedback

The Feedback button at the top of every page opens a small form. Nothing is sent until you press Send. Then the page sends your message, the email address if you gave one, and the address of the page you were on (its path, such as /learn/like.html, nothing after it). With them go a few facts that help fix a problem: the kind of device, its system and browser with their versions, whether it has a touch screen, the screen and window sizes, the browser's language, time zone and local time, day or night edition, and in the game the chapter, the mode and the part you are on (never your team name, answers or queries). They go to a Google Apps Script that adds one row to the course owner's Google Sheet, with the time it arrived, and emails the same message to the owner's inbox. Only the owner reads it, apart from the reply draft below. The email is there so the owner can answer you, and it receives one short automatic note from contact@alephb.uk saying your message arrived; leave it empty if you do not want an answer. If you gave an email, your message and the facts above (never the email address) are also sent to Anthropic's Claude API to draft a reply; the owner reads and edits every draft, and nothing is sent to you until the owner presses Send. Please do not put other personal details in the message. To have a message deleted, write to the address below.

Saved progress (signing in)

Signing in is optional and only for learning mode: it keeps a copy of your progress so you can continue on another device. The game's front page and the Sign in button at the top right of the game and the handbook's pages offer it. Google's sign-in script (from accounts.google.com) is loaded only when you press one of them, and Google then asks you to choose your account. The page receives a token from Google that says which Google account signed in, and sends it once to a Google Apps Script of the course owner. The script checks it with Google and keeps one thing from it, a code computed from your account's Google id with a secret key. It cannot be turned back into your id, and signing in never keeps your email address or name. The script answers with its own sign-in token, valid for 60 days, which your browser keeps in its local storage (ritz.account) and sends instead of Google's from then on.

You can also sign in with an email address and a password instead of Google. Your password never leaves your browser: the page turns it into a verifier (PBKDF2-SHA256, 200,000 rounds, mixed with your address) and sends only that. To create an account or reset a password, the page sends your address to the same script, which emails a 6-digit code to it from contact@alephb.uk through the owner's Gmail. The address is used to send that email and is not kept (unless you ask for news, below). While a code waits, for 15 minutes at most, the script holds only a code computed from it. The account's row holds a code computed from your address with the secret key, in place of the address, and one computed from the verifier with a random salt, in place of the password. Nobody can read your address or your password back from the Sheet. Each email sent also stays in the Sent folder of the owner's Gmail. After that, sign-in works as with Google: the same sign-in token and the same saved copy.

When you sign in, the script also sends your email address back to your browser: the one you typed, or the one Google confirmed for your account. Your browser keeps it in ritz.account, for the choice below. The script keeps no copy of it from signing in.

Emails about new games and updates

This is a separate choice, off unless you tick it: Email me about new games and updates, on the form that creates an account with your email, and in the dialog under the Saved button for any signed-in account. Only if you tick it, the script writes your email address, in plain text, to a separate tab of the owner's Google Sheet, with the account's code, the time you agreed, where you ticked it, and a code for the unsubscribe link. Nothing else about you is kept there, and your saved progress never holds the address. The owner uses it only to write about new games and updates to The Ritz Affair, about once a month at most. No such email has been sent yet, and the address is never sold or passed to anyone else. If a mailing service is used later to send those emails, this notice will name it first.

To stop, untick the box, use the unsubscribe link at the bottom of any such email (no sign-in needed), choose Delete my saved progress, or write to contact@alephb.uk. Each of them removes the address from the tab at once. The tab keeps only the account's code and the time you unsubscribed.

While you are signed in, the page sends a copy of your learning-mode save (ritz.learn: progress, answers, notes, queries and badges) and the exercises page's solved list and history (ritz.exercises, ritz.exercises.history) to that script after you solve a chapter or an exercise, earn a badge, or leave the page, and reads it back when a page opens. Compete progress, your statistics choice and anything else are never sent. The script keeps one row per account in the owner's Google Sheet: the code above, when the row was created and last updated, your browser's clock at the last update, and the saves. When two devices disagree, the save further along is kept, so a device that is behind never wipes your progress; New investigation also starts the saved copy afresh.

Sign out (under the Saved button at the top right of the game and the handbook's pages) forgets the sign-in on this device. Delete my saved progress, in the same place, removes your row from the Sheet at once and signs you out; the copy in your browser stays until you clear it.

What the page loads from elsewhere

  • The site is hosted on GitHub Pages, which, like any web host, may log your IP address.
  • Fonts come from Google Fonts, and the SQL engine (sql.js, for the game, the handbook's exercises page, and a lesson page once you press Try it here) from cdnjs; both see your IP address when your browser fetches them.
  • On every visit: Google's AdSense and consent scripts from pagead2.googlesyndication.com and fundingchoicesmessages.google.com, and the ads themselves from Google's ad servers, including googleads.g.doubleclick.net and tpc.googlesyndication.com; these see your IP address, and with your consent (by default outside Europe) set advertising cookies.
  • Outside Europe's time zones on every visit, in them only after your Yes: the Google Analytics script from googletagmanager.com, and its measurements sent to google-analytics.com (none once you refuse).
  • Only when you send feedback: the form's message to script.google.com (see Feedback above), which sees your IP address.
  • Only when you press Sign in with Google: Google's sign-in script and window from accounts.google.com. Only when you sign in with your email: the address and the password's verifier to script.google.com. And, while you are signed in, your saves to and from script.google.com (see Saved progress), which sees your IP address.

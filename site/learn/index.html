The Clerk's Handbook

By AlephB

Plain-English pages on the SQL that The Ritz Affair practises, for a first SQL course. Every example runs on a small bakery database, never the game's archives, so nothing here gives a chapter away. The game's terminal follows PostgreSQL's rules; the last page, The Grammar of SQL, sums them up.

Exercises

Practise: write the queries yourself, in the page.

SQL exercises with solutionsTwenty graded questions in three levels, from a first filter to a recursive chain: run your query in the page's terminal and it is checked against the question. Solutions stay hidden until you open them.

Grammar

Reference: the rules the game's terminal follows, each linked to the chapter that explains it.

  • The Grammar of SQL: grouping, quotes, types and conditions, done right.

Learning chapters

Read: one construct per page, with examples on the bakery database.

  1. The bakery database: the example data, with a script to recreate it.
  2. Tables, keys and the schema: keys, relationships, exploring a database.
  3. SELECT and WHERE: choosing columns and filtering rows.
  4. ORDER BY and LIMIT: sorting and keeping the top rows.
  5. LIKE and text patterns: searching text with wildcards.
  6. Dates and times stored as text or numbers: comparing, ranges, substr and CAST, and why ISO dates sort.
  7. GROUP BY, aggregates and HAVING: counting and summing per group.
  8. JOIN, LEFT JOIN and self-joins: combining tables and finding what is missing.
  9. NULL and missing values: three-valued logic, COUNT, COALESCE and the NOT IN trap.
  10. CASE WHEN: labels, buckets and conditional counts.
  11. Subqueries: scalar, IN, EXISTS, correlated and derived tables.
  12. UNION, INTERSECT and EXCEPT: stacking two results, the rows they share, the rows one lacks.
  13. Window functions: RANK and LAG: ranking rows and comparing with the previous one.
  14. CTEs and WITH RECURSIVE: named steps and following chains.
